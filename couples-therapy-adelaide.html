// 01 — What's different

The body in the room, times two

Couples therapy is a chance to take a thorough inventory of your relationship and find the places that could use some support. We look at communication, emotional awareness, attachment and the common dynamics couples fall into, so you can both recognise the patterns you keep repeating.

What I add is attention to the body. When a conversation turns, each of you has a reaction that starts well before the words do. One partner's jaw sets and their voice gets louder. The other goes quiet, looks at the floor, and is mentally halfway out the door. Neither of you chose that. It's two nervous systems doing what they learned to do under pressure.

In a session I'll sometimes slow things right down and ask each of you what's happening inside, in that moment. Where the tightness is. What you feel like doing. It's often the first time one of you has seen the other's reaction up close, as something happening in their body.

I won't take sides, and I won't push either of you faster than you can go. Any invitation to notice your body is optional, for both of you.

The beautiful dance

Relationship therapy is a beautiful dance. Learning the steps can feel clunky at times. When you start seeing the changes in yourselves and in each other, feeling listened to and understood, and clearer on your direction as a team, you might find you don't want to stop.

Room for both sides

Each of you gets time to be heard without being interrupted or corrected. I'm on the side of the relationship, which means I'll be curious about both of you and gently honest with both of you. We go at a pace that suits you both.

Your first three sessions

Starting couples therapy can feel like walking into the unknown. Here's the general shape of the beginning, so you both know what you're agreeing to.

  1. One

    Together, 90 minutes

    Your first session is with both of you. I'll hear how the relationship began, what brought you here now, and what each of you hopes will change.

  2. Two

    A closer look

    Next we look at what each of you brings: your own history, how you handle closeness and conflict, and what happens in your body when things get tense.

  3. Three

    Naming the cycle

    Then we map the pattern you get caught in as a pair, agree on what to work on first, and settle on how often to meet.

// 04 — Questions

What couples ask first

Do we both need to be on the free call?

No. Either of you can book it on your own, and plenty of people do. If you'd both like to join, that's welcome too. The call is for questions and a sense of whether we'd work well together.

What if we argue in the session?

That's okay, and it's often useful. An argument in the room shows us the pattern as it happens. I'll step in to slow it down, help each of you notice what's going on in your body, and make sure neither of you is left steamrolled or shut out.

Can we do couples therapy online?

Yes. Sessions run on Zoom at the same fees. It can suit couples with busy schedules or who live outside Adelaide. Confirm: whether partners can join from separate locations

Is it only for romantic couples?

No. I work with any two people: family members, friends, or anyone in a relationship they'd like to understand better.

How often would we come?

Usually weekly or fortnightly, and sometimes monthly once things have settled. We'll decide together and check in as we go.

What if one of us tells you something in private?

Everything you share stays confidential within the legal limits I'll explain in our first session. How I handle something one partner tells me privately is something I'll talk through with you both at the start.
