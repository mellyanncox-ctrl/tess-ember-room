  heading_id: why-h
  intro: |-
    <p>Maybe you're exhausted by constantly being there for everyone else. Maybe you're just beginning to wonder whether you're living from genuine choice, or from an autopilot instinct to keep everyone happy and pleased with you.</p>
    <p>You might love caring for people. You might genuinely want to say yes sometimes.</p>
    <p><em>The question is: can you tell the difference between a wholehearted Yes and a Yes that comes at the expense of yourself? Or have the two become so muddled that you struggle to know where your No even begins?</em></p>
    <p>Maybe you've practised saying no in your head, only to do exactly the same thing over again, feeling incapable of change and starting to resent those in your life.</p>
    <h3>Why is it so hard to stop?</h3>
    <p>Because for many, ‘people pleasing’ is the nervous system’s deeply ingrained way of staying safe, connected and accepted - and it shows up in your body well before it enters your conscious mind. That's why common clinical and cognitive remedies like “just say no” aren’t always enough</p>
    <p>It’s about learning to catch your hyper vigilance, hold yourself with the fear long enough to recognise what’s actually true for you - and then building the capacity to act on it.</p>
    <h3>Beyond Boundaries: The Missing Piece in Healing People Pleasing.</h3>
    <p>“People Pleasing” is the name we’ve all adopted for what is fundamentally a stress response – known as “Fawning”. It lives side by side the other more commonly known stress responses - fight, flight and freeze.</p>
    <p>The fundamental function of fawning is to sacrifice your own needs for the sake of the other.</p>

  heading: My Story and How We Got Here…
  heading_id: story-h
  intro: |-
    <p>Hi, I’m Tess, a somatic psychotherapist based in Adelaide, and the founder of The Ember Room. For nearly two years, I’ve worked in private practice with a diverse range of individual and couples clients, supporting people to better understand themselves, their relationships, their emotions and their needs.</p>
    <p>Last November I recognised that towards the end of the year, I often find myself exhausted and ready for a long nap, as my Celtic DNA yearns for winter around Christmas time… yet it is the time when our hemisphere is waking up and getting ready for silly season and the peak of social summer.</p>
    <p>I took myself through a month of deep self preservation and rest and called it ‘NOvember’. Where I gave myself permission for just one month to say no to absolutely everything and anything that wasn’t 100% obligatory and didn’t nourish my nervous system and general well being. I had an incredible experience, with all my health stats subsequently shooting through the roof, and went into the Christmas period the most resourced I can ever recall as an adult.</p>
    <p>Meanwhile, the theme of People Pleasing was coming up time and time again with clients. I found myself constantly recognising the same foundational pattern: that people pleasing starts as a fawning stress response, and requires someone to deny and suppress their own interoceptive responses to personal distress in order to prioritise connection and safety.</p>
    <p>Where other methodologies focus on cognitive concepts like boundaries and simply learning to say no, I saw the complexity of why people can’t assert their needs - because they’re often so disconnected from themselves they have no idea what those needs even are.</p>
    <p>So, I started dreaming up the idea to open NOvember to the public this year as a cute 4 week campaign to encourage people to say no and share about it. Alas, I couldn’t help myself, and it turned into a powerful and potent 4 week course that guides and supports people through essential teachings and practices to help them understand and start shifting their people pleasing tendencies.</p>
    <p>I am incredibly proud of what I’ve created and so look forward to helping you learn, and shift these long standing habits to find access to your no, so that you can fully live your yes.</p>
    <p>Tess xo</p>
