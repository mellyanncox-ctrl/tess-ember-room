// 01 — Why it's so hard to stop

It starts in the body, before you've had a chance to think

For many of us, people pleasing is the nervous system's deeply ingrained way of staying safe, connected and accepted. It shows up in your body well before it reaches your conscious mind.

That's why the common advice, "just say no", often isn't enough. By the time you think it, your body has already said yes, smoothed things over and checked the room.

The fawn response

You've probably heard of fight, flight and freeze. Fawning is the quieter fourth one: when the body's way of handling a threat is to appease, agree and keep the other person happy. It's usually learned early, and at the time it made sense. It isn't a diagnosis, and it isn't a flaw in your character. It's a strategy your body worked out to keep you connected.

So the work is learning to catch your hypervigilance, hold yourself with the fear long enough to recognise what's actually true for you, and then build the capacity to act on it.

A question to sit with

Can you tell the difference between a wholehearted Yes and a Yes that comes at the expense of yourself?

If the honest answer is "not really", that's fine. It's where most people start.

This is not about becoming selfish

I want to be clear about this one. Nobody is asking you to stop caring. This is about becoming someone who can care deeply about other people without abandoning yourself in the process.
